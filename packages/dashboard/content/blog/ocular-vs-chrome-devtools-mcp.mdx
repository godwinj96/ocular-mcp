## Your AI is working half blind

Ask your AI about a web page and it has two options. Guess from the code. Or ask you to go and look.

[Chrome DevTools MCP](https://github.com/ChromeDevTools/chrome-devtools-mcp) fixes half of that. It
borrows the Chrome browser sitting on your computer, so your AI can see whatever that browser
already has open.

Which leaves the other half. Most of the web is not open in your browser, and never will be.

## Ocular shows it the pages your browser can’t reach

A rival’s pricing page. A test link a workmate dropped in chat. The guide you are building from,
today’s version rather than the one your AI learned months ago.

Ocular opens the page itself and sends back what it saw. It does not need the page to be on your
computer, and it does not need you to open it first.

The same setup also covers the site you are building on your own machine. One tool, both halves, no
second thing to install and nothing to pick between when your AI needs a look. The cheap plan
reaches most of the wider web. Most of it, not all.

## What comes back is a picture and a map

Not a pile of code for your AI to guess from.

You get the page the way it really looks. With it comes a plain list of everything on it: what you
can see right now, what you would have to scroll down to find, and where each thing sits.

That second part matters more than it sounds. A page can be perfectly correct in the code and still
come out wrong on screen. Anything drawn rather than written, a chart or a game or a map, has no
code to read at all. A picture is the only way to know.

## Animations come back as one picture

Claude cannot watch video. Show it a moving image and it sees the first frame and nothing else.

So Ocular lays the frames out side by side in a single picture. Your AI sees the whole movement at
once, and it costs the same as any other picture.

## It looks. It doesn’t touch.

There is no clicking, no typing, no jumping to another page. Not turned off. Simply not built.

That means nothing can go wrong by accident, and there is nothing for you to switch off. It also
means Ocular is the wrong tool if you want your AI to click through a checkout and report what
broke. That job needs a tool that can press the buttons, and Chrome DevTools MCP is a fine one.

Anything that can change a page can also break one. That is just what that kind of tool is for. It
does not make one safe and the other not. Whatever either tool sees goes straight to your AI.

## What it costs

A few lines in a settings file. Nothing else to install.

$2.50 a month. No cap on the site running on your own computer. Out on the wider web you get 40 page
views a day, because that half runs on servers that cost money.

## When it earns its keep

Three ordinary moments. All of them end the same way today. Your AI asks you to go and look, or it
guesses from your description.

**A rival built something and you want to know how.** Their page runs on their machine, not yours.

**A workmate drops a test link in chat.** Your browser has never seen that page.

**Your AI needs today’s guide**, not the version it learned months ago.

None of this is unusual. It is the ordinary shape of a working week, and every one of them is a
point where your AI runs out of road.

## The short version

Your AI should be able to look at any page you can look at. Not just the ones already open on your
computer.

That is what Ocular is for. It looks, it does not touch, and one setup covers the site on your
machine and the wider web. $2.50 a month.
