## The one thing that splits them

Both tools do the same basic job. They let an AI helper see a web page.

They split on one question. Can your own browser already open that page?

[Chrome DevTools MCP](https://github.com/ChromeDevTools/chrome-devtools-mcp) uses the Chrome
browser that is already on your computer. So it can only see what that browser can already get to.

Ocular opens the page itself and sends back a picture of it. That includes pages your own browser
was never going to reach.

That is the whole difference. The rest is detail.

## What Ocular does differently

**One setup covers both sides.** The site you are building on your own computer. And pages out on
the wider internet. Same tool either way. You do not install a second thing for the half your
browser cannot open. The cheap plan reaches most of the wider internet. Most of it, not all.

**You get a picture, plus a map of what is on it.** Not a pile of code for the AI to guess from. You
get the page the way it really looks. With it comes a list of everything on the page, what you can
see right now, what you would have to scroll down to reach, and where each thing sits.

**Moving things come back as a strip of pictures.** Claude cannot watch video. Show it a moving
image and it only sees the first frame. So an animation comes back as one picture with the frames
laid side by side. It costs the same as any other picture, and it shows the whole thing.

**It can look, but it cannot touch.** There is no clicking, no typing, no jumping to another page.
Nothing can go wrong by accident. There is nothing to switch off. On your own computer there is no
cap on how much you use it.

## Where Chrome DevTools MCP is the better pick

It is made by Google. It is free. And a lot of people use it: 1.43 million downloads a week. If your
AI has looked at a web page lately, there is a good chance it used this.

It also does something Ocular cannot. Ten of its commands are for doing things to a page. Clicking,
typing, filling in forms. It can move between pages, wait for things to load, and check how fast a
page runs. If you want your AI to click through a checkout and tell you what broke, that is the
tool. Ocular was never built for that.

Ocular is not a cut-down version of it. It is built for a different job. Looking, not touching. You
do not need something that can click around on a page you were only ever going to read.

And anything that can change a page can also break one. That is simply what that kind of tool is
for. It does not mean one is safe and the other is not. Whatever either tool sees gets handed
straight to your AI.

## Their own words on the limit

The Chrome DevTools MCP guide is clear about this. It runs on **your own computer**, and it says so:
it is “designed to run on the same machine as the browser.” Reaching a browser somewhere else is
listed as something they might add one day. It is not there now.

So the limit is real. Point it at a page that is not running on your computer, and you are back to
describing that page to your AI instead of showing it.

## What it takes to set up, and what it costs

Both take a few lines in a settings file. Nothing else to install.

Chrome DevTools MCP is free. There is no cap to run into.

Ocular costs $2.50 a month. There is no cap on the site running on your own computer. Out on the
wider internet you get 40 page views a day, because that half runs on servers that cost money.

## When it pays for itself

Three ordinary moments. All of them end the same way today. Your AI asks you to go and look. Or it
guesses from your description.

**A rival built something and you want to know how.** Their page runs on their machine, not yours.
Nothing on your computer can open it for your AI.

**A workmate drops a test link in chat.** Your browser has never seen that page.

**Your AI needs today’s guide**, not the version it learned months ago.

None of this is unusual. It is the ordinary shape of a working week. Every one of them is a point
where a tool stuck on your own computer runs out of road.

## Which one you want

If your AI only ever looks at pages on your own computer, and you want it clicking around in them,
Chrome DevTools MCP is the right answer. And it is free.

Ocular is for the other half of the job. The page your AI cannot open by itself, and the site on
your own computer, from one setup. It looks, it does not touch. $2.50 a month.
